To find the right Odoo partner in Bangladesh, verify official partner status on odoo.com, check certified staff on Odoo 17 to 19, ask for references on Bangladesh-specific work such as Mushak 6.3 and 9.1 reports, VDS and TDS withholding, and payroll under the Labour Act 2006, confirm support hours in Bangladesh time, and read the contract for code ownership and milestone-based payment. This page does not rank or recommend companies. It gives you the evaluation framework the community uses, so that you can judge any firm or freelancer on fit rather than on marketing. The [partner directory](/partners) lists providers who have submitted their profiles; listing there is not an endorsement.

## The Bangladesh Odoo provider landscape

Three kinds of providers deliver Odoo work in Bangladesh:

| Provider type | Typical profile | Best suited for |
|---|---|---|
| Official Odoo partners | Firms listed on odoo.com with a partner tier, able to resell Enterprise licences and access Odoo support | Enterprise deployments, companies that want vendor-backed upgrades and a formal SLA |
| Independent firms and software houses | Local companies implementing Community edition, often with OCA modules and custom development | Community deployments, budget-conscious SMEs, teams that want local hosting |
| Freelancers and small teams | Individual functional or technical consultants, often ex-partner staff | Small scopes, specific tasks such as Mushak reports or payroll rules, support retainers |

None of these categories is better by definition. A Gold partner can assign a junior to your project; a freelancer with ten years of Bangladesh accounting work can deliver a cleaner setup. The point of evaluation is to find out who will do the work and whether they have done it before.

## Official partner tiers and what they mean

Odoo S.A. runs a partner programme with tiers that, at the time of writing, are Learning, Ready, Silver and Gold. The tier is driven by the number of certified consultants and the volume of Enterprise licences sold, not by customer satisfaction or by local localisation expertise. Use the tier as a signal of scale and commitment, not of quality. A firm can be an excellent Community implementer with no tier at all, because the programme only counts Enterprise sales.

What partner status does give you:

- The ability to buy Enterprise licences through a local invoice in BDT with 15 percent VAT, rather than by card in USD.
- Access to Odoo's support desk and the official upgrade service for Enterprise customers.
- Some assurance that staff have passed Odoo certification exams.

Check the partner directory on odoo.com directly. Do not rely on a logo on a website, because tiers lapse and are sometimes displayed after they have expired.

## Certifications: useful but limited

Odoo publishes functional certifications per version (for example Odoo 17 or Odoo 18 Functional). Ask how many people on your proposed team hold a current certification and on which version. Since a new version is released every October, a certification on a version two releases old is a mild concern.

Certifications test standard features. They do not cover Bangladesh localisation. For that, ask directly:

- Have you configured Mushak 6.3 invoices and Mushak 9.1 tax grids before? Can we see a sample report with the client's name removed?
- How do you handle VDS and TDS withholding on vendor bills?
- Have you set up payroll with festival bonus, provident fund, gratuity and overtime at double the basic rate?
- Have you worked with bKash, Nagad or Rocket settlement files, and with post-dated cheques?
- Have you supported a bonded warehouse or UD-based material tracking for an exporter?

A provider who answers these with specifics has done the work. One who answers with generalities has not, whatever their tier.

## References: how to check them properly

Ask for two or three references in your industry and size band, and actually call them. Useful questions for a reference:

1. Did the project go live on the planned date, and if not, why?
2. Did the final cost stay inside the original estimate? What changed?
3. Who did the work day to day, and are those people still with the firm?
4. How is support after go-live: response time, who answers, what it costs?
5. Would you use them again for the next phase?

Be cautious with references that are only logos on a slide. Named customers you cannot call tell you nothing. Be equally cautious if every reference is from a different industry; it suggests breadth of sales rather than depth in yours.

## Team, continuity and the "who actually works on it" question

The sales conversation is usually with a director. The implementation is done by consultants and developers. Ask for the names and experience of the proposed team, and whether they are employees or subcontractors. Staff turnover in Dhaka software firms is high, so ask what happens if your lead consultant leaves mid-project. A documented process, configuration notes and code in a Git repository reduce that risk; a project that lives in one person's head does not.

## Support hours, location and language

Confirm:

- Support hours in Bangladesh Standard Time, and whether Friday and public holidays are covered. A factory running Saturday shifts needs more than Sunday to Thursday office hours.
- Response and resolution targets by severity. "Same day" without a definition is not a target.
- Whether support is by ticket, WhatsApp, phone or on-site visit, and what each costs.
- Language: training and documentation in Bangla for warehouse and shop floor staff is often the difference between adoption and resistance.
- Where their hosting partners put your data. If you need data residency in Bangladesh, ask for a named local data centre.

## Contract terms to read carefully

| Term | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Scope | Written list of apps, users, reports, integrations and localisation items. Vague scope is the root of most disputes. |
| Odoo version and edition | Named in the contract. Agree what happens if a new version ships mid-project. |
| Payment schedule | Milestones (discovery signed off, configuration complete, UAT passed, go-live) rather than calendar dates. Avoid paying more than 30 to 40 percent before anything is demonstrated. |
| Change requests | A written process with a day rate and approval step. |
| Code ownership | Custom modules delivered in source form in a repository you control. You should be able to switch providers without losing your system. |
| Data and backups | Who holds backups, how often, and how you get a full database export on request. |
| Post-go-live support | Duration, hours, response times, and the monthly retainer after the included period. |
| Licence handling | If they resell Enterprise, whether the subscription is in your name and whether you can renew directly with Odoo. |
| Exit | Notice period and handover obligations, including documentation and credentials. |

A fixed-price contract with a clear scope suits small SMEs. A time-and-materials contract with a cap suits larger projects where scope will evolve. Either is fine; a contract with neither a clear scope nor a cap is the problem.

## Red flags

- A quote without discovery. Nobody can price a 20-user implementation accurately after a 30-minute call.
- Everything promised as "standard" including Mushak reports and payroll, with no custom development line.
- No mention of data migration effort or of your team's own time.
- Reluctance to give you the source code or database access.
- Pressure to buy Enterprise licences for every employee including those who will never log in.
- A demo on a generic dataset only, with no willingness to run a proof of concept on your hardest process.
- A price far below the community-observed ranges (BDT 3 to 8 lakh for a small SME, BDT 8 to 25 lakh for mid-size, BDT 25 lakh to 1 crore plus for garments or manufacturing). Someone is going to pay for the gap, usually you, later.

## A simple scoring sheet

Score each provider from 1 to 5 on the eight criteria below, weight the ones that matter most to you, and compare. The exercise matters more than the arithmetic: it forces the same questions to every provider.

1. Verified partner status or equivalent track record on Community.
2. Certified staff on Odoo 17 to 19.
3. Bangladesh localisation references (VAT, Mushak, withholding, payroll).
4. Industry references you were able to call.
5. Named team and continuity plan.
6. Support hours, channels and response targets.
7. Contract clarity: scope, milestones, code ownership, exit.
8. Line-item estimate against your written scope.

Run your scope through the [ERP readiness checklist](/resources/erp-checklist) first so that each provider is pricing the same thing. The [implementation guide](/implementation-guide#discovery) explains what a proper discovery phase looks like, which is a fair test of any provider before you commit.

## Working with a freelancer

Many successful Bangladesh deployments are delivered by one or two experienced individuals. To make that work: insist on written scope, weekly check-ins, configuration documentation, code in a repository you own, and a defined support arrangement after go-live. Ask who covers for them during illness or travel. For a 5-user company this model is often the most cost-effective; for a 150-worker factory it concentrates too much risk in one person.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I verify that a company is an official Odoo partner?

Search the partner directory on odoo.com, filtered by Bangladesh. Official partners are listed with their tier (Learning, Ready, Silver or Gold) and the number of certified staff. If a firm is not listed there, it is not an official partner, although it may still be a competent implementer of Community edition.

### Do I need an official partner to implement Odoo?

No. Odoo Community can be implemented by any developer or firm, and many Bangladesh projects are delivered by independent freelancers or in-house teams. Official partner status matters mainly for Enterprise licences, access to Odoo support and the vendor's upgrade tooling.

### What does an Odoo certification prove?

Odoo functional certifications (for example v17 or v18) show the person passed the vendor's exam on standard features. They do not prove Bangladesh localisation experience, so ask for Mushak, withholding and payroll references as well.

### What do Odoo partners in Bangladesh charge?

Community-observed day rates range from about BDT 8,000 to 15,000 per person-day, with experienced freelancers and small firms at the lower end and established partners with a bench at the upper end. A small SME implementation commonly lands at BDT 3 to 8 lakh, mid-size at BDT 8 to 25 lakh. These are ranges, not quotes.

### Should I pick a partner or a freelancer?

For 2 to 10 users with standard scope, a documented freelancer engagement is often cost-effective. For 30 or more users, multi-site operations or garments and manufacturing, a firm with several consultants and a support desk reduces continuity risk.
